# Lecture: Intelligence Gathering and Conduct Outside of Uniform

**Presented by Officer Ancarion Saelthar**

Recruits and soldiers,

<!--page-->

Many of you believe that serving the Dominion begins when you put on your armor and ends when you remove it. This is false. A soldier represents the Dominion at all times, whether in full armor, common clothes, a tavern, a marketplace, or while traveling between holds.

A careless soldier can do more damage to the Dominion than an enemy ever could.

## Part I: The Purpose of Intelligence

Information wins battles before swords are ever drawn.

<!--page-->

A single report can reveal a Talos gathering, a Daedric cult, an assassination plot, a shortage of strategic resources, political disputes between holds, or opportunities beneficial to the Dominion.

Your task is not merely to hear information. Your task is to observe, remember, and report. Always ask yourself: Who? What? Where? When? Why? How?

<!--page-->

A report that says, "A man was praising Talos," is nearly useless. A report that says, "An elderly Nord male with grey hair wearing green common clothing was openly praising Talos near the Whiterun market at midday," is useful. Details matter.

## Part II: Observation

When entering a settlement, observe before speaking. Take note of new arrivals, armed individuals, political and religious discussions, unusual gatherings, distinctive clothing, and strange behavior.

<!--page-->

Many individuals reveal more by what they do than what they say. An individual constantly looking over his shoulder may be afraid. An individual asking too many questions may be gathering intelligence. An individual trying too hard to appear harmless may be concealing something. Observe first. Speak second.

<!--page-->

## Part III: Speaking With Locals

Do not interrogate everyone you meet. Most useful information is volunteered freely. Be polite. Listen more than you speak. Allow others to tell their stories. People often reveal valuable information when they believe they are simply having a conversation. A soldier who talks constantly learns very little. A soldier who listens learns everything.

<!--page-->

## Part IV: Conduct Outside of Uniform

When out of uniform, your purpose is observation, not attention. Do not boast about the Dominion. Do not announce your rank. Do not reveal internal matters. Do not discuss ongoing investigations. Do not identify informants. Never identify yourself or another individual as an informant in public. Loose tongues bury good intelligence. If someone provides information, protect their identity.

<!--page-->

## Part V: Security

Assume that someone is always listening — the tavern keeper, the merchant, the traveler, the beggar, the enemy. Never discuss sensitive matters where strangers can hear them. Never reveal patrol schedules, guard rotations, embassy security, informant identities, or internal disputes. Once spoken, information cannot be recalled.

<!--page-->

## Part VI: Rumors and Facts

Not everything you hear is true. Record facts separately from rumors. Fact: "An Argonian named Sees-in-Dark was present in Riften." Rumor: "Sees-in-Dark is organizing attacks against the Embassy." The first can be verified. The second requires investigation. Good intelligence officers know the difference.

<!--page-->

## Part VII: Final Words

The greatest intelligence asset is not a spy. It is a disciplined soldier. A disciplined soldier observes, remembers, reports accurately, and knows when to speak and when to remain silent. Every recruit here carries the reputation and security of the Dominion with them wherever they travel. Conduct yourselves accordingly.

**For the Dominion.**
